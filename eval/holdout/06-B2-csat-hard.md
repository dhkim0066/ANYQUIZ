**Ⅰ. 빈칸 추론 — 1~3번 / 각 12점, 총 36점**

**1. 다음 빈칸에 들어갈 말로 가장 적절한 것은? [12점]**

A school cook hopes to add dishes that students would enjoy but have never had the opportunity to try. She invites them to write suggestions on cards, yet nearly every card names something already sold near the school. A student can name an unfamiliar dish that a friend has described, but cannot request a flavor that has never come to mind. Counting the cards would therefore give the cook a precise answer to a narrower question than the one she intended to ask. Instead, she offers a few unfamiliar dishes in small portions and collects cards again at the end of lunch. Some of the new requests are for dishes that no student had mentioned when the first cards were collected. The students are much the same, but they now have something different on which to base their answers. To discover what students might enjoy beyond the present menu, the cook must first ______.

① separate regular customers from students who rarely eat there  
② ask students to defend dishes that resemble earlier suggestions  
③ give students experiences from which new preferences can form  
④ measure how firmly students hold their present preferences  
⑤ reserve unfamiliar dishes for students who request them first  

**2. 다음 빈칸에 들어갈 말로 가장 적절한 것은? [12점]**

A company testing a repair service pays technicians for every case they close before the end of the day. At first the shorter lists of open cases appear to show that customers are receiving faster help. Looking more closely, a manager notices that several apparently separate cases concern the same machines and the same faults. A technician who suspects a second problem has little reason to investigate it once the machine briefly starts working. Leaving the case open overnight would make careful work look slow, whereas closing it now produces an immediate reward. The morning technician can open another case if the same customer calls back with the same complaint. Each decision makes sense under the rule, yet together the decisions keep customers waiting for a lasting repair to a fault that never really went away. The difficulty arises because the reward ______.

① favors workers who accept unusually complicated repairs  
② gives equal weight to customer reports and technical advice  
③ measures a problem before anyone has attempted a repair  
④ depends on failures that technicians have no way to observe  
⑤ treats an administrative ending as evidence of a practical one  

**3. 다음 빈칸에 들어갈 말로 가장 적절한 것은? [12점]**

When an actor lowers her voice during an argument, listeners may hear anger, kindness, or a warning in the same quiet words. A student trying to copy the performance often measures the pause and matches the volume, yet still produces a different effect. The missing element may lie several lines earlier, where the original actor made an ordinary promise sound unusually confident. Against that promise, her later quietness seems like the first sign that confidence has begun to fail. Without it, the quiet line merely sounds like another example of the character's generally careful speech. The teacher therefore asks the student to change the earlier promise while keeping the later line exactly as before. Listeners now hear a change in the character at precisely the point where no fresh adjustment was made. In performance, the force of a single choice can depend on ______.

① the number of different emotions that a line can express  
② expectations established before the audience hears the line  
③ an actor's ability to repeat a gesture at equal intervals  
④ a close match between ordinary speech and written dialogue  
⑤ the length of time listeners have studied the complete play

**Ⅱ. 글의 순서 — 4~5번 / 각 11점, 총 22점**

**4. 주어진 글 다음에 이어질 글의 순서로 가장 적절한 것은? [11점]**

> Two groups of students want to compare how quickly water disappears from two kinds of garden soil after rain. They fill identical pots, pour the same amount of water into each, and weigh the pots every afternoon.

(A) The first results suggest that one soil loses water faster, until someone notices where those particular pots were standing. By chance, all of them were on the side of the room that receives warm afternoon sunlight.

(B) Those two comparisons reveal that both kinds dry faster near the window, while neither consistently dries faster than the other. What the first arrangement appeared to show about the soil was largely a consequence of the positions assigned to the pots.

(C) To separate the possible effects of soil and sunlight, the students place some pots of each kind on each side. This new arrangement allows them to compare different soils in one location and identical soils in different locations.

① (A)–(C)–(B)  
② (B)–(A)–(C)  
③ (B)–(C)–(A)  
④ (C)–(A)–(B)  
⑤ (C)–(B)–(A)  

**5. 주어진 글 다음에 이어질 글의 순서로 가장 적절한 것은? [11점]**

> A choir learning a piece from an old recording keeps making the final word of each line unexpectedly long. Listening to the recording again does little to settle whether these long endings really belong to the music.

(A) When the second recording is played back, the note seems to continue after every singer has already stopped. The difference suggests that part of what the choir heard in the old performance may have come from the building.

(B) With that possibility in mind, the singers try shorter endings in their own hall and listen from different seats. They are now using the earlier performance to guide an effect, while allowing their own surroundings to influence how they produce it.

(C) The director proposes a way to test a different explanation by making two recordings of a single short note. The singers perform it in a small room and then in a wide stone hall, stopping together on her signal.

① (A)–(C)–(B)  
② (B)–(A)–(C)  
③ (B)–(C)–(A)  
④ (C)–(A)–(B)  
⑤ (C)–(B)–(A)

**Ⅲ. 문장 삽입 — 6~7번 / 각 11점, 총 22점**

**6. 글의 흐름으로 보아, 주어진 문장이 들어가기에 가장 적절한 곳은? [11점]**

> For the smaller streets, however, that silence had a different and much simpler explanation.

Imagine a historian who hopes to learn which town streets flooded during an especially wet winter many years ago. ① She finds a notebook in which a road worker recorded the condition of the main streets every morning. ② When an entry for one of these streets contains no mention of water, she reasonably treats the silence as evidence that it stayed dry. ③ The worker's daily route never took him along them, so he could have missed even a fairly serious flood there. ④ Before marking any street as dry on her map, the historian therefore checks whether the worker actually passed it. ⑤ Her map ends up with fewer definite answers, but its empty spaces now express something important about the limits of her source. A missing report can tell us about an event only if someone was in a position to report it.

**7. 글의 흐름으로 보아, 주어진 문장이 들어가기에 가장 적절한 곳은? [11점]**

> The second group, by contrast, heard the same greeting each time a person entered, whatever the reason for the arrival.

To understand how a foreign greeting is used, students may need examples that show when speakers choose something else. ① Consider two groups in a language class who heard a short greeting equally often during a series of lessons. ② The first group heard it when a speaker entered a room to begin a visit and start a conversation. ③ Sometimes a speaker simply returned to collect a forgotten coat, in which case the class heard a different expression. ④ Comparing these arrivals gave the first group a reason to connect the greeting with the start of a social visit. ⑤ For them, even getting the coat provided another example of the greeting, so entering the room appeared to be the only condition. What the groups could learn depended on which differences their examples made visible, although both had heard the target expression equally often.

**Ⅳ. 요약문 완성 — 8번 / 10점**

**8. 다음 글의 내용을 한 문장으로 요약하고자 한다. 빈칸 (A), (B)에 들어갈 말로 가장 적절한 것은? [10점]**

When a basket maker teaches an apprentice, she may deliberately leave a small gap between two steps in her demonstration. She finishes the first row, puts down the basket, and asks the learner to predict where the next strip should go. An uninterrupted display would show the correct movement more quickly, but it would also hide a useful distinction from the learner. Predicting forces him to choose among several paths that seem equally reasonable when somebody else is doing the work. If his choice is wrong, the next movement answers a question that he now understands well enough to ask. If he is right, he can still compare the reason for his choice with the reason the expert gives. The pause takes time, yet it changes what the apprentice can see when the demonstration continues. A movement once remembered only as a shape now becomes an answer to a problem he has actually faced.

> A pause that invites a learner to (A) ______ the next step can turn a familiar movement into a solution the learner can (B) ______.

① anticipate — explain  
② imitate — question  
③ record — repeat  
④ anticipate — overlook  
⑤ imitate — explain

**Ⅴ. 함축 의미 — 9번 / 10점**

**9. 밑줄 친 부분이 다음 글에서 의미하는 바로 가장 적절한 것은? [10점]**

An office that makes desks for schools decides to study how high its customers prefer their writing surfaces to be. Its designers measure the desks that several schools already own and find that most are close to the same height. The agreement looks encouraging until a teacher recalls that the schools bought their desks from one old catalog. Its printer had room for only a few models, and the maker had removed the less common heights to save space. The designers have therefore measured the results of choices that were limited long before any pupil sat down to write. If they use that agreement to set the height of their next model, <u>the old catalog will get a vote twice</u>. It will have shaped the furniture in yesterday's classrooms and then quietly entered today's decision as if it expressed the pupils' present wishes. A sensible next step would be to let pupils try writing at several heights that the old catalog never offered.

① The old maker will receive two separate orders for the same product.  
② Past limits on choice will influence a new decision through the evidence collected.  
③ The schools will be asked to compare two editions of the same catalog.  
④ Present preferences will correct an earlier assumption about the available models.  
⑤ Pupils will have their individual choices counted once at each school.
