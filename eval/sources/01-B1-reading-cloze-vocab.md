**[1~4] 다음 글을 읽고 물음에 답하시오.**

Last spring, Yuna noticed that her neighbors often threw away things that could still be repaired. She asked the building manager for permission to put a “repair shelf” near the entrance. Residents could leave small broken objects there.

Mr. Han, a retired engineer, volunteered to help every Saturday. At first, Yuna wanted to fix everything. However, Mr. Han explained that some objects were too dangerous for them to repair. They decided to accept only simple, safe items.

Whenever they fixed something, they returned it to its owner. Yuna also kept a notebook of their repairs. She used ⓐ**it** to remember what she had learned.

After six weeks, they had repaired twenty-seven objects. More neighbors began visiting the shelf, and some offered to teach their own skills. “We are saving more than objects,” Yuna said. “We are learning to ask for help and share what we know.”

*resident: 주민 / retired: 은퇴한*

**[5~8] 다음 글을 읽고 물음에 답하시오.**

At the beginning of the semester, Jiho decided to read twenty pages every day. He followed his plan for three days. Then he became busy with homework and missed two days. He thought he had failed, so he stopped reading.

His teacher suggested a smaller goal: reading for ten minutes after dinner. Jiho tried again. On busy days, he read only two or three pages. He marked the days he read on a calendar but did not record the number of pages.

One evening, he was so tired that he fell asleep without reading. This time, however, he opened his book the next day instead of giving up.

A month later, Jiho finished his first book of the semester. “I used to think that I had to follow my plan perfectly,” he said. “Now I know that missing one day does not erase all my progress.” He felt confident that he could continue his new habit.

*semester: 학기 / erase: 지우다*
