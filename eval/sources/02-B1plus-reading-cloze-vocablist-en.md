**Ⅰ. READING COMPREHENSION**

**[1–3] Read the following passage and answer the questions.**

To choose a route for a school walking event, Hana’s team initially compared only distance. A navigation app recommended a road that was 600 meters shorter than another option, so the decision seemed obvious. However, their teacher asked them to walk both routes at the same time of day as the planned event.

The shorter route had almost no shade and crossed three busy streets. The team had to stop several times to wait for traffic. The longer route passed through a park and had only one street crossing. Although it required more walking, the team reached the destination feeling less tired.

In their final report, the students compared comfort and safety as well as distance. These factors changed their choice: they recommended the longer route. Distance remained useful, but they no longer treated it as the only measure of a good route.

Hana ended the report with a question: “A number can be accurate and still give an incomplete picture. Before accepting a ranking, shouldn’t we ask what it measures and what it leaves out?”

① How Navigation Apps Calculate Distance  
② Choosing a Route: Looking Beyond Distance

④ Planning a School Event Without Technology  

**2. Which statement is NOT true according to the passage? [12 points]**

② The longer route passed through a park.

**3. What can be inferred from Hana’s final question? [12 points]**

**[4–6] Read the following passage and answer the questions.**

Students preparing a local history exhibition found an old photograph of a crowded street. Their first assumption was that the people were celebrating a festival. They almost wrote this explanation on the label without checking it.

However, a newspaper from the same year mentioned a protest against the closing of a train station. This was possible evidence for a different interpretation, although the students could not prove that the newspaper described the event in the photograph.

They displayed two possible interpretations, both clearly labeled as tentative, and invited visitors to contribute observations of their own. ⓐ**This approach** led to discussions about details the students had overlooked.

One visitor identified a shop sign that helped the students narrow down the date of the photograph. Yet this information was still not enough to support a final conclusion about why the crowd had gathered.

By making uncertainty visible, the students turned the exhibition into a shared investigation. They learned that a useful explanation does not always have to be a complete one. It can also show what is known, what remains unclear, and what evidence should be sought next.

*protest: 항의 시위*

**4. What is the main idea of the passage? [12 points]**

① Historical exhibitions should display only photographs with complete records.  
② Visitors’ memories are always more reliable than written documents.  
③ Acknowledging uncertainty can encourage further investigation.  
④ A photograph’s date is more important than the event it shows.  

**5. What does the underlined phrase ⓐThis approach refer to? [12 points]**

① Removing the photograph until its meaning could be proved  
② Presenting possible explanations and inviting visitors’ observations  
③ Accepting the students’ first explanation without checking it  
④ Asking visitors to choose one explanation through a vote

② Uncertain information has no place in a public exhibition.  
③ Finding one useful clue is enough to settle every question.

3. **①** — 순위가 정확한 수치에 근거하더라도 사용자에게 중요한 요소를 빠뜨릴 수 있다.
4. **③** — 불확실한 부분을 공개하자 관람객의 참여와 추가 조사가 이루어졌다.
5. **②** — 두 가지 가능한 해석을 제시하고 관람객에게 관찰한 내용을 보태도록 한 방식을 가리킨다.
6. **④** — 새 단서로 사진의 촬영 시기는 좁혔지만 군중이 모인 이유까지 확정하지는 못했다.
7. **①** — 토론 전에 혼자 생각을 적으면서 다른 사람의 첫 제안에 영향을 받기 전에 자기 생각을 발전시켰다.
8. **③** — 자연스러운 번역을 위해서는 개별 단어뿐 아니라 관계와 상황에 담긴 의도도 이해해야 한다.
