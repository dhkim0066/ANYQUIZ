**Ⅰ. 독해**

**1. 다음 글의 요지로 가장 적절한 것은? [12점]**

A school wanted to learn whether its new outdoor study area was successful. At first, teachers counted how many students entered it during lunch. The number rose every week, and the project was quickly described as a success. However, conversations with students revealed a more complicated picture. Some visited only because their friends were there; others left after a few minutes because there was no shade. A smaller group stayed quietly and used the space exactly as intended. The entrance count treated all these visits as equal, even though they represented very different experiences. The teachers therefore began asking how long students stayed, what they did, and what prevented them from returning. These questions gave the original number a context. When we evaluate a project, the easiest thing to count may be only a small part of what matters. A useful measure should be connected to the purpose of the activity, rather than chosen simply because it is convenient.

① 성과 측정의 신뢰도를 높이려면 평가 항목을 단순화해야 한다.  
② 공공 공간의 사용 목적은 방문자 개인의 선택에 맡겨야 한다.  
③ 사업을 평가할 때는 목적에 맞는 지표와 이용 맥락을 함께 고려해야 한다.  
④ 이용자의 만족도를 높이기 위해서는 시설의 규모를 확대해야 한다.  
⑤ 이용자 수가 꾸준히 증가하는 시설에 예산을 우선 배정해야 한다.

**2. 다음 글의 제목으로 가장 적절한 것은? [12점]**

Many reading recommendations begin with a simple question: What have you enjoyed before? The answer can be helpful, but it can also keep sending a reader toward familiar subjects. A small library tried a different approach. Once a month, it placed books from unrelated sections on a single table without grouping them by topic. A visitor looking for a travel book might encounter a collection of poems or a guide to building simple machines. The table was not designed to predict anyone’s taste accurately. Its purpose was to create meetings that a carefully filtered list might prevent. Some books were ignored, of course, and not every unexpected choice became a favorite. Yet several visitors said they had discovered interests they would never have known to search for. The experience suggests that choosing only what already matches our preferences can limit what those preferences become. A little room for surprise allows curiosity to develop beyond the boundaries of our previous choices.

① Why Readers Need Clear Book Categories  
② Predicting Taste through Past Reading Choices  
③ The Hidden Costs of Changing Library Displays  
④ Reading More by Choosing Familiar Subjects  
⑤ Unexpected Books Can Open Up New Interests

**3. 다음 글에서 “Your script is a map, not the journey.”가 의미하는 바로 가장 적절한 것은? [12점]**

Before leading her first walking tour, Nora memorized every sentence in the guidebook. She knew the dates of the buildings and the names of the people who had lived in them. During the tour, however, a child asked why one house had two front doors, and an older visitor wanted to rest before the next stop. Neither situation appeared in her notes. Nora kept speaking as planned, but she could see that the group was losing interest. Afterwards, an experienced guide told her, **“Your script is a map, not the journey.”** He explained that notes could prepare her for a tour without determining every moment of it. On her next tour, Nora carried the same information but paid closer attention to her visitors. She allowed their questions to influence where she paused and what she explained. Her preparation became more useful when she learned to adjust it to the people in front of her. Knowing the material was only the beginning of knowing how to guide.

① A guide should memorize the route before learning historical details.  
② Prepared knowledge should be adapted to the needs of actual visitors.  
③ Visitors learn more when they explore a place without a guide.  
④ A successful tour follows its original plan as closely as possible.  
⑤ Written information should be replaced with personal stories.

**4. Student Audio Story Festival에 관한 다음 안내문의 내용과 일치하지 않는 것은? [12점]**

Greenhill Library is inviting students aged fifteen to eighteen to join its Student Audio Story Festival. Participants may enter individually or in pairs, and each entry must be an original story lasting between three and five minutes. Stories may be fictional or based on personal experiences, but they must be suitable for younger listeners as well. Registration closes on May tenth, and completed recordings must arrive by May seventeenth. Selected stories will be played at a public listening event on May twenty-fourth. There is no participation fee. Students who need recording equipment may borrow a microphone from the library for two days, although advance booking is required because supplies are limited. Judges will consider the clarity of the story and the creative use of sound rather than the price of the equipment used. Every participant will receive written feedback, and three selected teams or individuals will be invited to discuss how they developed their stories at the event.

① 15세부터 18세까지의 학생이 참가할 수 있다.  
② 개인 또는 2인 팀으로 참가할 수 있다.  
③ 출품작은 길이가 3분에서 5분 사이인 창작 이야기여야 한다.  
④ 마이크는 사전 예약 없이 빌릴 수 있다.  
⑤ 모든 참가자는 서면 피드백을 받는다.

**5. 주어진 글 다음에 이어질 글의 순서로 가장 적절한 것은? [12점]**

> When a school decided to create a small rooftop garden, students first chose plants from photographs in a catalog. They preferred colorful flowers and expected the roof to look like the pictures within a few months.

**(A)**  
With this revised list, the students finally ordered their plants. The garden looked less colorful than their original drawing, but most plants remained healthy. They had learned that an attractive plan becomes practical only when it responds to the place where it will be used.

**(B)**  
Before they placed the order, however, the caretaker asked whether they knew how hot and windy the roof could become. The students realized that the catalog showed each plant under suitable conditions. Could those same plants survive in the very different conditions on their roof?

**(C)**  
To answer that question, they recorded the temperature and wind on the roof for a week. They also checked how quickly water disappeared from several test pots. Using these observations, they replaced some delicate flowers on their list with plants better suited to the actual site.

① (A)－(C)－(B)  
② (B)－(A)－(C)  
③ (B)－(C)－(A)  
④ (C)－(A)－(B)  
⑤ (C)－(B)－(A)

**6. 글의 흐름으로 보아, 주어진 문장이 들어가기에 가장 적절한 곳은? [12점]**

> **Before lifting these flaps, visitors were asked to observe the objects silently for thirty seconds.**

At a local museum, staff noticed that visitors often walked straight to the labels beside the objects. **( ① )** Many read the printed explanation and then left without looking closely at the object itself. **( ② )** The staff wondered whether information was arriving too early, giving visitors a conclusion before they had formed a question. **( ③ )** They tested a different arrangement in one room, placing the same descriptions behind small paper flaps. **( ④ )** This brief delay encouraged visitors to examine details and develop an interpretation of their own. Some compared an object’s shape with familiar tools, while others discussed what it might have been used for. **( ⑤ )** When they finally read the label, they had something to compare with the museum’s explanation. The information now answered a question they had begun to ask. The staff kept the labels, but changed the point at which visitors encountered them.

*flap: 한쪽이 붙어 있어 들어 올릴 수 있는 덮개*

**Ⅱ. 빈칸 추론**

**7. 다음 빈칸에 들어갈 말로 가장 적절한 것은? [14점]**

An architecture club used to keep only photographs of its completed models. The unfinished drawings were thrown away once a project was over. When new members joined the following year, they admired the photographs but struggled to understand why certain choices had been made. They even repeated a design that an earlier group had rejected because it blocked the entrance. The club then began saving rough sketches together with short notes about the problems each one had raised. These records included ideas that had seemed promising but were too expensive or difficult to build at the time. Later members could see which limits still applied and which had changed. Sometimes an abandoned idea became useful when different materials were available. The purpose of preserving these imperfect records was to **____________________**. A finished product showed what one group had achieved; the earlier sketches helped another group decide what might be worth trying next.

① make earlier reasoning available for future decisions  
② present the club’s best results in the most attractive form  
③ help new members repeat successful designs without alteration  
④ separate creative ideas from practical limits during planning  
⑤ make the final appearance of every project predictable

**8. 다음 빈칸에 들어갈 말로 가장 적절한 것은? [14점]**

People who know a place well can forget how much knowledge their directions require. A resident might tell a visitor to turn left at the old cinema, even though the building now contains a clothing store and has no sign of its former use. To the resident, the instruction seems perfectly clear. To the visitor, the most important landmark is invisible. Adding more details about the old cinema may only deepen the confusion. What the visitor needs is a feature that can be recognized without knowing the neighborhood’s history. The same problem can occur when an experienced person explains a task to a beginner. Familiar terms may contain assumptions that the speaker no longer notices. Effective communication therefore requires us to **____________________**. We need to imagine which parts of our explanation depend on experiences the other person has never had. An explanation becomes useful when it provides a starting point that both people can actually share.

① replace unfamiliar local names with technical descriptions  
② consider what the listener does not already know  
③ provide every detail we remember about the subject  
④ preserve the original wording of familiar instructions  
⑤ choose examples that are most memorable to the speaker
